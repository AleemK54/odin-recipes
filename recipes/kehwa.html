Description

Kehwa (or Kahwa) is the traditional green tea of the Swat Valley and the broader Pashtun region. It is a symbol of hospitality and is served to guests as a welcoming gesture. Unlike standard black tea, Kehwa is light, highly aromatic, and packed with warming spices like cardamom and cinnamon. It is usually served without milk, often with a touch of sweetness and sometimes a few slivers of almonds. It is the perfect drink to end a heavy meal like Rosh or Kabuli Pulao.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups water
  • 1 to 2 teaspoons loose green tea leaves
  • 3 to 4 green cardamom pods, slightly crushed
  • 1 small piece of cinnamon stick
  • 2 to 3 whole cloves
  • 1 small slice of fresh ginger (optional)
  • A pinch of saffron strands (optional, for special occasions)
  • Sugar or honey to taste
  • A few slivered almonds for garnish (optional)

Steps

  1. In a small saucepan, bring the water to a boil.
  2. Add the crushed cardamom pods, cinnamon stick, cloves, and ginger (if using). Let the spices boil in the water for 3 to 4 minutes so the water becomes fragrant.
  3. Add the green tea leaves and reduce the heat to low.
  4. Let the tea simmer gently for another 3 to 5 minutes. Do not boil it too long, or the tea will become bitter.
  5. Add the sugar or honey and stir until dissolved. If you are using saffron, add the pinch now.
  6. Turn off the heat and strain the tea directly into small cups or a teapot.
  7. Garnish with a few slivers of almonds if desired, and serve hot.
